# Psychological Copy Contract

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Scope:** Reflection summaries, generated synthesis, emotion descriptions, inferred needs,
somatic explanations, and crisis-adjacent copy.

## Product Boundary

Emot-ID supports emotion vocabulary and self-reflection. It does not diagnose, assess immediate
risk, infer causes, or establish what a person feels. Model output is a set of possibilities. The
user remains the authority on their experience.

This contract governs English and Romanian together. A review is incomplete until both languages
express the same uncertainty, agency, safety boundary, and action level.

## Required Principles

1. **Possibility, not finding.** Use "may", "can", "could", "one possibility", or an invitation to
   notice. Do not present a generated label, need, body association, or explanation as fact.
2. **User authority.** Make room to reject, revise, or ignore every interpretation. Do not reward
   agreement or frame disagreement as resistance.
3. **Observation before explanation.** Describe model placement or commonly reported experiences
   separately from possible meaning. Never turn correlation into an individual causal claim.
4. **No diagnosis or risk inference.** A word, map point, body sensation, pattern, or combination
   cannot establish a disorder, severity, danger, or self-harm intent.
5. **Needs are options.** Offer needs and next steps for consideration. Do not say that a generated
   result proves what the user needs or should do.
6. **Choice and stoppability.** Exercises are optional, can be skipped, and should say when to stop
   if they may intensify discomfort.
7. **Nonjudgmental language.** Avoid universal claims such as "healthy", "toxic", "normal",
   "abnormal", or claims about worth, maturity, resilience, or self-awareness.
8. **Proportionate directness.** Ordinary reflection stays tentative. Verified crisis resources
   may use direct action language when the user reports possible immediate danger or self-harm;
   selected labels alone never justify that conclusion.

## Approved Patterns

- "X is one of the suggestions. Does that fit?"
- "X can sometimes involve..."
- "One possibility to consider is..."
- "This describes placement in the model, not cause or severity."
- "Keep only what fits your experience."
- "The word alone cannot show whether you are in danger."
- "If you may be in immediate danger, contact emergency services."

Romanian equivalents should preserve the same force:

- "X este una dintre sugestii. Se potrivește?"
- "X poate include uneori..."
- "O posibilitate de luat în considerare este..."
- "Poziția în model nu stabilește cauza sau gravitatea."
- "Păstrați doar ce se potrivește experienței voastre."

## Prohibited Patterns

- "You are experiencing X" when X was generated rather than directly stated.
- "Your body/system is telling you..." or "Your body needs..."
- "X means...", "X proves...", "X is a signal that...", or "X is caused by..."
- "You need..." based only on a label or model result.
- Diagnostic labels, treatment claims, or promises of healing.
- Claims that one exercise resets, regulates, heals, or fixes the nervous system.
- Universal claims that an emotion or combination is healthy, destructive, adaptive, or evidence
  of a personal trait.
- Crisis escalation that treats selected words as evidence of present danger or self-harm intent.

## Somatic Boundary

Published bodily maps describe group-level self-reports, not a diagnostic map for an individual.
Body copy must distinguish:

- what the user directly selected;
- a group-level or catalog association;
- a possible interpretation;
- an unknown cause.

Do not infer an emotion from a sensation without showing it as a revisable suggestion. Do not claim
that a location, side of the body, or intensity establishes psychological meaning.

## Crisis Exception

Crisis tiers and combinations remain deterministic, auditable prompts for making support visible.
They are not assessments. Crisis copy must:

- state that labels cannot determine danger or self-harm intent;
- keep verified resources visible before tier-4 reflection details;
- allow direct emergency language only as a conditional ("if this is true" / "if you are unsure");
- preserve the user's ability to continue to reflection after acknowledging the support prompt.

Changes to crisis rules, thresholds, combinations, ordering, or gating require explicit safety
tests and a separate review.

## Review Checklist

- English and Romanian changed together.
- Generated language uses uncertainty and preserves rejection.
- No diagnosis, causal certainty, trait judgment, or unsupported physiological mechanism.
- Suggested need/action remains optional and context-dependent.
- Crisis directness is conditional and does not infer danger from labels.
- Somatic claims identify their evidence level and limitations.
- Unit tests cover contract regressions; Playwright covers the rendered critical journey.
- Source, reviewer, scope, and unresolved limitations are recorded in the iteration log.

## Evidence Basis

- [SAMHSA, Trauma-Informed Approaches and Programs](https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-approaches-programs):
  safety, trust, collaboration, empowerment, voice, and choice.
- [World Health Organization, Psychological First Aid](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241548205):
  humane, supportive, practical help that respects dignity, culture, and abilities without pressure
  or false promises.
- [Nummenmaa et al., Bodily maps of emotions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3896150/):
  group-level self-reported bodily sensation maps; not an individual diagnostic instrument.
- [Systematic review of diagnostic uncertainty communication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9971421/):
  supports explicit, patient-centered communication of uncertainty.

These sources inform interaction and copy principles. They do not validate Emot-ID as a clinical
instrument.
